# Coast run: build notes

Living record of the game and the 3D model it uses. Update this file whenever
either changes. The numbers here mirror `src/game/constants.ts` and
`src/game/loop.ts`; if they ever disagree, the code is right and this file
needs fixing.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `index.html` + `src/main.ts` | The game. Was `highway.html` in the prototype. |
| `supercar.html` + `src/viewer/main.ts` | The 3D model viewer (orbit, inspect, download OBJ + MTL or GLB). Links to the game. |
| `src/car/buildCar.ts` | Shared model builder: `buildCar({ paint })` returns a `THREE.Group` of named meshes. |
| `src/car/mergeCar.ts` | Merges a built car into one geometry per material, for instancing. |
| `src/styles/tokens.css`, `src/assets/fonts/` | Coast run's tokens (palette sampled from the scene) and Archivo. |
| `public/` | Favicon, touch and Android icons, web manifest, social card, wordmarks. Copied into the build as is. |
| `src/world/` | Road ribbons, baked sky, terrain and sea, roadside props, lights. |
| `notes/VISUAL-BRIEF.md` | Scale plan, palette, light values and budgets for the scenery. |
| `docs/` | The built site that GitHub Pages serves. Generated by `npm run build`; never edit it by hand. |

## The car

Built from primitives, nose pointing to -z, modelled in metres: 4.6 m long,
2.24 m wide (mirrors included), 1.24 m tall, resting at y = 0.

- Body is a side-profile `Shape` extruded across the width, then run through
  `taper()`, a `widthFactor(y, z)` that narrows the nose, tail and roof and
  adds fender bulges over both axles. That function is what makes the slab read
  as a car; everything else is positioned relative to it.
- The body extrusion has **no bevel** on purpose: the painted surface is exactly
  the profile polyline, so glass, lamps and fascia parts can be offset a known
  2 to 3 cm along their own surface normal and stay proud of the paint.
- Named parts: `body`, `windshield`, `engine-cover`, `side-window-*`,
  `wheel-*` (tire / rim / hub / spokes), `front-splitter`, `rear-diffuser`,
  `diffuser-fin-*`, `intake-*`, `skirt-*`, `rear-wing` + struts + endplates,
  `headlight-*` + housings, `front-grille`, `grille-fin-*`, `nose-intake-*`,
  `taillight-*`, `mirror-*`, `exhaust-*`.
- Seven shared materials: `paint` (parameterised), `carbon`, `glass`, `rubber`,
  `alloy`, plus the `lamp` and `taillight` emissives. Names carry into the
  OBJ/GLB export, so the download opens in Blender with usable part and
  material names.
- The hex colours are the handoff's. Surface response follows measured ranges
  now that there is an environment to reflect: paint is a dielectric under a
  clearcoat (roughness 0.34, clearcoat roughness 0.06), alloy is a full metal
  (roughness 0.28), glass is near-mirror at 74% opacity. Traffic uses plain
  gloss paint without the clearcoat.
- The body's normals are smoothed across creases under 25°, so the hood, roof
  and floor shade as curved panels while the real edges (29° and up) stay
  crisp. No vertex moved.
- In the game the player's wheels roll and the front pair steers (0.32 rad at
  full lock), the taillights brighten under braking, and two blue flames
  trail the exhausts during a boost. Those live in `carFx.ts`, so the model
  the viewer exports stays static.
- It is an original mid-engine supercar design, not a replica of a specific
  marque's bodywork. Keep it that way.

## The game

Endless coastal highway, freeplay, three strikes.

**Road shape.** `curve(d) = 15·sin(d/210) + 9·sin(d/560 + 1.3) + 7.5·sin(d/88 + 0.6)`.
The third term is what gives real turns. `bend(d)` is its second difference
over ±14 m and pulls the car toward the outside of a corner, so turns have to
be steered: `x -= bend(dist) · speed · 20 · dt`.

**World.** The player never moves forward: `S.dist` advances and the world is
drawn relative to it. Road, shoulders and guardrails are ribbons, strips of
vertices sampled every 3 m from 24 m behind to 300 m ahead, whose x is rebuilt
each frame from `curve()`. The same function places traffic, pads, coins,
obstacles, skid marks, marker posts and the minimap, so everything bends
together. The road surface is a generated 512×1024 canvas texture (edge lines,
dashed dividers, double amber centre line, aggregate specks, wheel tracks,
worn paint) with a matching roughness map. It repeats every 32 m and scrolls
at road speed. The guardrails are W-beam section, 0.46 to 0.77 m above the
road, on posts every 4 m.

**Coast.** The land bends with the road, so the shoreline follows the highway.
To the left the verge falls away over a bluff to a beach, which slides under
the sea (4.5 m below the road) about 22 m beyond the shoulder, with a foam
line at the water's edge. To the right a cut bank rises 1 to 6 m, then open
hillside climbs to about 50 m. Palms, rocks and scrub are scattered by a
fixed hash of track distance, so a given stretch always looks the same.
White marker posts with royal-blue caps every 26 m on both shoulders.

**Light.** One late-afternoon sun, 30° up and 60° left of the road, over the
sea. The sky (gradient, sun, clouds, far ridges) is painted once into a cube
map at start-up and used both as the backdrop and as the image-based light.
Fog runs from 120 to 285 m in the sky's horizon colour. Tone mapping is
Khronos PBR Neutral at exposure 0.8, so the car's paint matches its swatch.

**Lanes.** Four lanes at x = -6, -2, +2, +6. The right pair (+2, +6) runs the
player's direction. The left pair comes head-on. The run starts in the +2 lane.

**Driving.** Accelerate 11 m/s², brake 26 m/s², coast drag 3.2 m/s², minimum
8 m/s. Top speed 66 m/s (about 238 km/h), 90 m/s (about 324 km/h) on boost,
easing back to the cap at rate 1.6. Steering input is smoothed at rate 9 and
moves the car at `steer · (5.2 + speed · 0.045)` m/s. Guardrails clamp x at
±9.0 m; touching one scrubs speed by 1.5% a frame and leaves skid marks.

**Traffic.** A pool of 13 cars in five paints. 60% of spawns go the player's
way: lanes +2 or +6, 235 to 295 m ahead, 17 to 26 m/s. 40% are oncoming: lanes
-6 or -2, 300 to 360 m ahead, 20 to 28 m/s toward the player. A car never
spawns within 34 m of another in its lane. The spawn interval eases from
2.4 s to about 1.25 s (plus up to 0.7 s of jitter) over the first 150 s and
stays there.

**Pickups and hazards.** Coin arcs of six, 9 m apart, drifting up to about 1.25 m
across a player-side lane, every 5 to 10 s: +45 each. Boost pads every 7 to
13 s in any lane: +120 and 2.6 s of extra top end. Cones (6) and concrete
barriers (3) sit in the player-side lanes every 6.5 to 10.5 s early on,
tightening to 4 to 8 s; obstacles never spawn within 40 m of a car in their
lane.

**Collision.**

| Against | Hit box (half extents) | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Car | 4.3 m long, 2.15 m wide | strike, speed × 0.42 |
| Cone | 2.6 m, 1.4 m | strike, speed × 0.5 |
| Barrier | 2.6 m, 2.2 m | strike, speed × 0.5 |
| Coin | 3.2 m, 1.9 m | +45 |
| Boost pad | 4.3 m, 2.15 m | +120, 2.6 s boost |

A strike shakes the camera and gives 1.6 s of invulnerability with the car
blinking at 12 Hz. The third strike ends the run.

**Scoring.** 0.55 points per metre, ×1.6 while boosting. Passing a car within
3.4 m sideways: +60, or +110 if it was oncoming. Best score persists in
`localStorage` under `coast-run:best`.

**Camera.** Low chase: 8.2 m behind, closing in with speed, height
1.45 m + speed · 0.006, lateral lag on the car, slight roll into steering, FOV
56° to 78° with speed. On portrait screens (aspect below about 0.65) the FOV
widens to hold a 38° horizontal view and the camera follows the car more
tightly, because the landscape tuning cannot keep an outer lane in frame.

**Audio.** WebAudio, built on the first input: two detuned oscillators through
a lowpass for the engine (pitch and cutoff follow speed) and band-passed noise
for wind above 25% of top speed.

**HUD.** Coast run tokens throughout: white cards, Archivo, the surf-blue accent.

- *Desktop:* stats card top-right (Score, Coins, Best, Time), strikes
  top-centre, speed gauge bottom-right, minimap bottom-left with the link back
  to the model below it.
- *Strip layout* (windows under 760 px wide or 560 px tall, and all touch
  play): Score, Coins and Time in one strip top-left, strike dots top-right,
  gauge and minimap at two-thirds size. Best is dropped from the strip; it
  still shows on both overlays.
- *Touch:* the gauge and minimap move up under the strip so the bottom corners
  are free for the thumb zones. A sound button sits top-right. The corner link
  to the model viewer moves into the start and run-over panels.

**Input.** Keyboard, touch and gamepad feed one set of controls (see the table
in `README.md`). Touch uses two zones rather than four buttons: the left zone
steers and the right zone holds Brake and Gas, and a thumb can slide between
the two buttons in a zone without lifting. Gamepad triggers are analog, so a
half-pressed RT gives half the acceleration; keys and touch are all or
nothing, exactly as in the prototype. A hit rumbles the controller, or buzzes
a phone that supports it.

**Rendering.** Traffic is drawn as seven instanced meshes (one per car
material, the paint carried as an instance colour), coins as one, marker posts
as two, and each kind of roadside prop as one or two. The player and the
viewer use the full car with its named parts. A full scene is about 100 draw
calls and 155,000 triangles. One 2048 px shadow map (1024 on touch devices)
covers 30 m around the player.

**Quality fallback.** If a second of frames averages under about 42 fps, the
game steps down once: render resolution to 1.5×, 1.25×, then 1×, then the
shadow map to 1024, then shadows off. It never steps back up.

**Randomness.** Gameplay uses `Math.random`. Everything visual uses the
hash and seeded generator in `world/noise.ts`, so scenery cannot change a
run, and the parity test against the prototype keeps working.

## Identity

- **Name.** Coast run. Searched before identity work (2026-10-08): no game,
  app or software product by that name. The nearest marks are a pending
  "Coastal Run" (athletic retail, class 35) and a dead "Coast Runner"
  (inflatable boats, class 12).
- **Wordmark.** Archivo at width 125, weight 800, tracking -0.01em. The word
  space is the device: the road's double amber centre line seen from the car,
  two bars narrowing toward the horizon (9 units apart at the base, 4 at the
  top, 0.72 em tall). Outlined in `public/wordmark-light.svg` and
  `wordmark-dark.svg`.
- **Icon.** The device alone on asphalt `#2C3242`, the bars running off the
  bottom edge. Same drawing at every size from 16 px up.
- **Palette.** Sampled from the scene: night `#0E1B30` (text), asphalt
  `#2C3242`, surf `#1F5BD6` (the one accent), sky `#9DC0F8`, sand `#E6D9BC`,
  amber `#E8A03C`, red `#D8443C`, foam `#F5F7FA`. Text pairings are noted
  with their ratios in `tokens.css`.
- **Paints.** Surf, Night, Sky, Sand, Foam.

## Open

- Traffic wheels do not spin (the cars are merged for instancing). The
  player's do.
- Frame rate on real phones is unmeasured. The quality fallback was tested by
  driving the frame loop with slow timestamps, never on a slow device.
- Oncoming traffic has no headlight glow, and the barrier is still a plain box.
- No pause. The frame clamp absorbs a tab switch, but a touch player cannot
  stop mid-run.
- On a 320 px wide phone the top strip and the strike dots sit edge to edge.
