How to play

Six shapes. Adjust each one until it looks right, then find out where the maths sits and where practice sits.

Those two are rarely the same place. A circle the same height as a square looks smaller. A block centred by coordinates sits low. Everyone has heard this. Almost nobody can say by how much.

  • Nailed it

    Inside the band that working practice covers.

  • Close

    Right direction, not quite far enough, or a little past.

  • Off

    Wide of the band, or adjusted the wrong way entirely.

No measurement is shown while you adjust. A number on screen turns this into counting to a target rather than judging by eye.

A plumb line finds true by gravity rather than by eye, and plumb centre means exactly centred. The whole game is the gap between that and what looks right.

There is no single correct answer to an optical adjustment, which is why the target is a band. Your record tracks something more useful than accuracy: whether you move the right way at all, and whether you stop short.
